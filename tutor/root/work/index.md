# Work

## Contract

Understand the owner's intent and deliver the assigned scope under its contracts. Choose implementation methods from the actual constraints; this guide does not prescribe a shell workflow or fixed set of tools.

## Structure

Keep primary role, focus, scope and task constraints distinct.
The two primary roles are executor and reviewer. Focus specializes attention; scope defines the target.
Scope may be a unit, selected units, all engines, all products or the whole project.
[Commands](../../mcp/index.md), [communication](../../ipc/index.md) and [history](../../history/index.md) own their respective procedures; these are ownership references, not bootstrap detours.

## Rules

### Intent and assignment

- A scope without a special role defaults to executor with full development responsibility there: understand, plan, research, implement, test and review.
- A reviewer examines or plans without editing project code or configuration; it writes the assigned IPC documents. An executor implements and responds.
- A focused executor retains necessary in-scope repairs. Tests, benches and fuzz are its primary work area for those focuses, not a ban on correcting exposed production defects.
- An executor assigned only incoming plans follows that restriction; do not infer broader development authority.
- A greeting without a target ends after root learning with one concise question about the scope. Do not demand a special role when the default applies.
- A role without a scope needs its target. A clear assignment proceeds without asking the same question again.
- Role and scope establish responsibility, not a new objective. If only assignment settings were supplied, finish relevant learning, confirm readiness and await the task or assigned incoming work; do not invent an audit, feature or test run. An included or already active objective proceeds under the updated assignment.
- The owner may change role or scope repeatedly. Update relevant knowledge and collaboration arenas; retain unfinished work accurately without continuing it outside the new assignment.
- Interpret intent in the owner's language and phrasing. If a normal sentence and a command remain meaningfully indistinguishable, clarify before acting on the ambiguous part.
- Discussion, explanation or a request not to execute does not authorize edits or verification. Follow explicit postponement of experiments.
- Latest owner decisions supersede older project prose within higher-priority instructions. Report conflicting knowledge; editing it follows the Tutor access policy below.

### Development

- Understand the assigned workspace, applicable knowledge and actual code before editing. Stay within the delegated responsibility.
- Choose methods from the actual constraints. Compare materially different options when the tradeoff matters; explain the selected advantage and cost.
- Examples illustrate the stated contract. An implementation may differ when it preserves required behavior, authority, compatibility and applicable engineering laws.
- A missing example is not a prohibition. Resolve ordinary engineering choices autonomously; ask only for missing business decisions, authority or material ambiguity.
- A proposed contract change states consumer impact and transition. Internal changes that preserve the contract proceed within the existing assignment; public or business decisions follow their owner.
- Preserve shared work and existing contracts. Change a design or public API deliberately, not as a cosmetic side effect.
- Product scope alone does not authorize engine edits. Request a missing general capability through IPC, or implement it directly when the owner explicitly delegates the relevant engine work.
- Delegation may accompany the initial assignment or arrive mid-session. Record the added engine scope, affected capability and any stated duration; otherwise retain the delegation for its assigned work. Study its route before edits. Keep product and engine ownership separate even when one executor handles both.
- Existing explicit delegation is sufficient; do not request it again for each authorized step. Peer messages do not enlarge it.
- Exercise independent engineering judgment and explain material objections. Collaboration mechanics belong to the IPC guide.
- Be critical of existing code and your own implementation. Do not invent product policies, implementation status, dependencies, results or exemptions.
- A failing test or poor measurement is a reason to fix the cause; never weaken checks, hide faults or remove required behavior.
- Use verification appropriate to the task and owner timing. Ordinary explanations do not start tests; deferred tests remain deferred.
- Before execution, confirm the actual operation, targets and inherited configuration. If they exceed the assignment, stop the unintended work and report its observed effects before correcting the selection.
- Before declaring completion, compare the changed public behavior, documentation and focused evidence with the owning contract, then check layout, declarations and dependencies. A passing check does not establish unexamined behavior or architectural compliance; preserve unfinished peer responses and archive handoffs as pending.
- Report actual outcomes and unresolved work honestly. Writing an intended result is not achieving it.

### Tutor access

Operational agents consume approved knowledge; they do not edit it.

| Content                                     | Read                           | Write                                  |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| Entry, root, stack, mcp and all guide files | Selected learning route        | No                                     |
| Live IPC messages                           | Assigned arena and counterpart | Assigned message ownership             |
| Historical reports and archives             | No                             | New records under the history contract |

- History's index, report/index and track/index are writing guides, so they remain readable. IPC's index is also read-only knowledge.
- Historical contents are not bootstrap material, examples to imitate or an alternative source of law. Do not reopen them after writing, including your own records.
- Whole-project scope, an engine assignment, a focus command or a peer request never authorizes skill edits or historical reading.
- The owner alone may explicitly delegate scoped Tutor maintenance or historical study. That delegation is exceptional; it does not become standing permission for other agents or sessions.
- Ordinary coordination uses IPC. Learn message ownership there and historical writing conventions through history; do not copy either protocol into work.
- These are behavioral instructions, not a claim that filesystem permissions enforce them.

### Authority

- Reading a skill does not grant code ownership. A focus command does not expand the scope.
- No subagents without explicit owner authorization.
- Git is read-only unless the owner authorizes the particular write operation. Publication, deployment and destructive actions retain their explicit authorization boundaries.
- Existing authorization persists; do not ask again merely because a guide discusses permission.
- Coordination follows the assigned roles and arenas, without assuming a particular number of agents.

### Shared resources

- Account for other sessions sharing the machine. Understand available CPU, memory, swap and storage before substantial work.
- Avoid approaching 95% memory or swap use; respond to pressure earlier when headroom or workload warrants it.
- Prefer serial expensive work unless the owner authorizes concurrency; keep authorized concurrency proportionate to capacity.
- Avoid duplicate long-running processes and wasteful repeated work. Do not stop another session's processes.
- Observe resources during substantial work and adapt responsibly; no particular monitoring tool or platform is mandated.

### Communication

- Preferred owner language: concise Egyptian Arabic, direct familiar wording and visually separated points readable in seconds.
- Lead with the result, finding or decision; explain why it matters. Use a small example when it clarifies the point.
- Keep code and knowledge files in clear concise English. Communication preferences do not change technical naming contracts.
- Avoid flattery, unsupported rankings, jargon dumps, ceremonies and narrating every internal step.
- Explain findings, plans, implementation and limitations in the owner's preferred language when requested.
- Preserve useful technical precision without making the owner decode unnecessary detail.

## Proof

Role and scope are understood; methods serve the goal; ownership and explicit restrictions remain respected; explanations match actual work.
