# Reports

## Contract

Record meaningful lessons after a significant round, deviation, difficult failure or substantive discovery.
Routine successful edits do not require a report.

## Structure

Mirror the actual responsibility, such as engine/<name>, engine, product/<name>/<part>, product/<name>, part/<name> or another explicitly assigned knowledge scope.
For a whole-project report, the scope may be empty so the date follows report/ directly. A selected-set scope has a stable name and states its exact members inside.
Use `history/report/<scope>/<YYYY-MM-DD>/NNN.md`.
The increasing sequence is local to that scope and day; preserve every existing name.
One cross-cutting report may reference known scoped records; do not clone their narratives or read history to assemble one.

## Rules

Use this order:

1. Context: task, owner, scope, event dates, source revision when known and intended outcome.
2. Evidence: actual work and observations; distinguish verified outcomes from expectations.
3. Deviations: failures, changed assumptions and rejected hypotheses.
4. Lessons: transferable findings, supporting observations and limits.
5. Closure: unresolved questions and the proposed knowledge owner; no self-approved new law.

No secrets, private customer data, giant logs or unsupported achievement claims.
Publish complete records. A correction is a new dated, numbered record identifying the earlier record; never rewrite the earlier evidence.
A candidate lesson remains a candidate until the owner authorizes knowledge maintenance.

## Proof

The author distinguishes observation, interpretation and uncertainty without consulting historical contents.

## Sources

Owner report design.

## Routing

Return to history after selecting the report owner. Apply its daily organization and root/work's access policy.
